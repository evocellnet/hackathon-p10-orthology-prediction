# S5: synteny (gene-order context) evidence, phase 1

Status: phase 1, 2026-10-26, T+2h00 to T+2h50 (times from `scripts/clock.sh`). This phase built the gene-order tables, ran the standalone species-pair sub-benchmark (OrthoBench dev and toy), implemented the ancestral adjacency and `context_support`, and previewed integration through the core's `NodeContext` hook. The core files (`merge.py`, `scoring.py`, `pipeline.py`, `families.py`) were not edited.

All numbers come from `results/S5_synteny/*.json`. The tables in §3 to §5 were generated by `scripts/summarize_S5.py` and are also written to `results/S5_synteny/tables.md`. Input and code checksums are in `results/S5_synteny/manifest.json`.

## 0. Summary
- **Coverage.** Gene-order tables cover OrthoBench at 100% for 10 species, 99.99% for human (3 proteins missing) and 99.996% for mouse (1 missing).
  - Alt-haplotype and patch genes are projected onto their primary chromosome: 2,959 of 3,051 in human, 4,437 of 4,721 in zebrafish and 342 of 350 in mouse.
  - QfO 2022: 920,254 of 988,778 proteins are placed (0.931). Mean coverage by domain: Archaea 0.998, Bacteria 0.985, Eukaryota 0.937 (median 0.996).
  - Gaps (§1.2): zebrafish 0.64, rat 0.83, amphioxus 0.08, Phaeosphaeria ≈0.
- **Signal on real data.** On OrthoBench dev, the synteny context score separates orthologs from paralogs among homologous pairs in vertebrate species pairs.
  - ROC-AUC is 0.64 to 0.93 per pair, and 0.75 pooled at w=10 (95% CI 0.71 to 0.80).
  - The shuffled-order control gives AUC 0.497 to 0.505 in every pair and window (negative control passes).
  - Human/fly: there is no signal (AUC 0.50), as expected after about 600 My of rearrangement.
- **Hard cases** are queries where the sequence score ranks the best paralog at or above 0.9 × the best ortholog: teleost ohnologs, 2R paralogs and tandem copies.
  - Sequence top-1 accuracy on them is 0.50 (n=157).
  - Synteny (w=10) decides 70 of these queries with precision 0.84. It abstains on the rest, mostly ties, including tandem duplicates.
  - Sequence plus synteny re-ranking within 0.75 × the best sequence score reaches 0.66: **+0.16, 95% CI [+0.05, +0.26]**, bootstrap over RefOGs.
  - Over all 644 queries that have both an ortholog and a paralog candidate (a selection that does not use labels), the gain is +0.023 to +0.026 at the 0.9 / 0.75 ratio, with CI lower bounds of about 0.
  - The shuffled control gives −0.006 to 0.
  - Dr/Tn hard queries: synteny is 22 correct, 0 to 1 wrong, 25 abstain.
- **Toy positive control.** On the in-house simulator, the signal is real but weaker than on vertebrates.
  - Leaf pairs: hard AUC 0.68 vs shuffled 0.50.
  - Ancestral levels (2 sims, every internal node): w=5 AUC is 0.66 to 0.91 (hard 0.51 to 0.86), against shuffled 0.49 to 0.51. Anchor precision is ≥ 0.995.
  - The toy has 80% tandem duplications and about 0.07 rearrangements per gene per unit branch length, so its gene order decays faster than vertebrate gene order. The toy is therefore a lower bound, not a calibration.
- **Phase 2 (§8), OrthoBench DEV pair-level, core merge with R1-only synteny.** Pair F1 rises from 0.730 (off) to 0.762 to 0.776, i.e. **+0.032 to +0.046** with CIs > 0. Δ vs shuffled is the same, and shuffled is null. Vert–teleost (WGD) gains +0.07 to +0.09. The keep rule passes.
- **Integration preview** (unmodified core merge, 8 toy sims, §5).
  - A multiplicative bonus (λ=0.1) halves the FP paralog pairs: 0.44× of `off`, against 0.91× for the shuffled arm. That is a real precision effect, with P going from 0.989 to 0.995.
  - It also costs recall (R 0.920 → 0.880, ΔF1 −0.019 against −0.005 shuffled), because the bonus inflates cross-child scores and so breaks the R2 in-paralog comparison.
  - The "override" bonus, which forces the partner choice, is harmful: FP 1.2 to 1.6×, worse than its own shuffled arm.
  - Own and core adjacency give identical merges.
  - The decisive test is OrthoBench dev in phase 2, together with an R1-only bonus hook (§5, §6).

## 1. Gene-order tables (`ancestra/genomes.py`, `ancestra/genomes_qfo.py`)
Build: `scripts/build_gene_order.sh` writes `data/genomes/orthobench/<species>.parquet` and `data/genomes/qfo2022/<UPid_taxid>.parquet`, plus a `coverage.tsv` in each directory.

There is one row per *input protein*. The schema is `genomes.COLUMNS`:

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `protein_id` | protein ID, as in the FASTA |
| `gene_id` | gene ID |
| `contig` | contig |
| `start`, `end` | gene span, not CDS |
| `strand` | ±1 |
| `order` | 0-based rank on the contig among the table's genes, sorted by start/end/gene_id. It is gap-tolerant: genes not in the proteome are skipped. |
| `contig_n` | number of table genes on the contig |
| `primary` | contig is in the primary assembly |
| `source` | data source |

OrthoBench tables also have `raw_contig` and `projected`.

### 1.1 OrthoBench (Ensembl 99 GFF3)
- **Mapping.** CDS `protein_id` → transcript → gene, using the `chr_patch_hapl_scaff` GFF3 for human, mouse and zebrafish. C. elegans OrthoBench IDs are `WBGene…​.1`, so they are matched to the gene ID after stripping `.1` (20,191 proteins).
- **Alt haplotypes** (required, because OrthoBench contains them; DATA.md §2).
  - Contigs that are absent from the primary GFF3 are non-primary. Examples: human `CHR_HSCHR6_MHC_*` (318 contigs, 3,051 genes), zebrafish `CHR_ALT_CTG*` (845 contigs, 4,721 genes), and mouse patches (63 contigs, 350 genes).
  - For each alt contig, gene symbols are matched to *unique* primary-assembly genes. The majority chromosome is taken, plus the median offset.
  - Each alt gene is placed at its own symbol's primary position, or failing that at alt start + offset. Its `order` is the insertion rank among the primary genes, so ties with the primary copy are allowed. Neighbourhoods are defined on `order`.
  - Alt contigs with fewer than 2 symbol matches stay on their own contig: 92 genes in human, 284 in zebrafish and 8 in mouse.
  - Ranks of primary genes are unaffected by the projection.

## 2. Standalone S5 sub-benchmark (species pairs)
**Script.** `scripts/S5_pair_benchmark.py`. Truth labels come from `scripts/S5_refog_labels.py`, which is benchmark-only code and is never imported by the method.

**Truth.** Labels come from DEV RefOGs only (34 RefOGs, `splits/orthobench_split.json`).
1. Read the curated tree `Supporting_Data/Data_for_RefOGs/trees_tight/RefOGxxx.tre`.
2. Root it on the edge that minimises species-overlap duplications. All leaves count, including outgroups. Ties go to the most balanced root.
3. Prune to the RefOG genes of the 12 species.
4. A cross-species pair is an ortholog iff its LCA is a speciation node by species overlap.
5. Pairs between different RefOGs of the same homology component are paralogs ("deep").

Result: 958 of 960 RefOG genes are placed. RefOG055 has 2 genes that are absent from its tree. Leaf names are parsed robustly: `Genus_species_ID`, bare `ID`, and `ID_2` suffixes. Over all 12 species this gives 4,485 ortholog pairs, 14,661 within-RefOG paralog pairs and 7,058 deep paralog pairs.

**Scores.**
- **Anchors** are genome-wide *unambiguous* reciprocal best hits: the best hit to any other gene must score < 0.9 × the best, on both sides. They come from DIAMOND `--sensitive -k 100 -e 1e-3` on the two whole proteomes (`scripts/S5_diamond_pairs.sh`, 14 to 46 s per direction at 8 threads).
  - Anchors whose genes are in the S0 family of a or b are ignored (default S0 `2sA_dc30_cc_sh30_RA`). This avoids circularity, and tandem copies never count.
  - Anchor counts: 15,045 for human/mouse, 11,405 human/chicken, 7,477 human/zebrafish, 8,477 zebrafish/Tetraodon, 3,702 human/fly and 8,178 human/Tetraodon. The WGD species lose the most anchors to the ambiguity filter: plain RBH gives 11,073 for human/zebrafish.
- **Context score** = the number of anchors (x,y) with x within ±w genes of a and y within ±w genes of b. `w ∈ {5, 10, 20}`.
- **Sequence score** = max DIAMOND bits over both directions, and 0 if not reported (19 to 45% of candidates, mostly distant paralogs).

**Hard queries.** A query gene in species A qualifies if it has ≥1 ortholog and ≥1 paralog candidate in B, and its best paralog bitscore is ≥ 0.9 × its best ortholog bitscore. "Hard pairs" are all candidates of hard queries.

**Reading the tables.**
- The pooled AUC of the *sequence* score is not a fair comparator, because raw bits are not comparable across families. Its CI is wide (0.59 to 0.89). The query-level top-1 tables are the relevant comparison, since that is the merge's decision.
- Larger windows give higher AUC but lower decision precision: 0.83 (w5), 0.84 (w10) and 0.73 (w20).
- Synteny abstains on most tandem-competitor queries (`abstain_tandem`). This is the pre-registered behaviour.
- The shuffled control has a small *negative* bias in the query rule with w=20 (precision 0.16 when it decides). The reason: the best of a larger paralog set is more often > 0 by chance. The real minus shuffled contrast is therefore conservative.

## 3. Ancestral adjacency and `context_support` (`ancestra/synteny.py`)

### 3.1 Representation (DESIGN §3, S5)
- **Leaf neighbour lists.** Computed once per genome by `genomes.neighbour_csr(contig_code, order, w)`. This is CSR over integer gene IDs, holding all genes within ±w ranks on the same contig (ties allowed). Cost is O(N·w).
- **Unit adjacency at node X.** `AncestralAdjacency.unit_adjacency(gene_unit, n_units)`.
  - It maps every leaf neighbour pair (g,h) through gene → unit, i.e. the ancestral gene of a child of X.
  - Units u ≠ v are adjacent iff some member genes are neighbours in ≥1 species. This automatically means within a child.
  - The result is a sparse CSR (deduplicated, symmetric). The cost is one vectorised pass over the clade's neighbour pairs.
- **Unit family.** The S0 family of the unit's genes. Merges never cross families.
- **Anchors (pass 1, sequence only).** `select_anchors(ca, cb, cs, child, amb=0.9)` gives 1:1 mutual-best cross-child unit pairs whose runner-up in the other child scores < 0.9 × the best. Alternatively, the core's own strict 1:1 mutual best (`NodeContext.anchors`) can be used.
- **Support.** `context_support((ca, cb), (pa, pb), node_adjacency)` counts, for each candidate (A,B), the anchors (P,Q) with P adj A and Q adj B. Anchors where fam(P) or fam(Q) is the family of A or B are excluded.
  - It is a numba kernel: per candidate, it scans adj(A) and the anchor partners of each neighbour, then binary-searches adj(B).
  - Cost is O(Σ deg(A) · log deg(B)).
  - At a leaf pair it equals the species-pair score of §2 exactly; it is the same kernel.

## 4. Integration API (for the merge owner)
`merge.run_merge(..., context=f)` now calls `f(ctx: NodeContext) -> bonus[len(ctx.cand_a)]` (core change made by the core owner). `ancestra.synteny.SyntenyContext` is such an `f`:

```python
from ancestra.genomes import to_gene_order            # my tables -> core GeneOrder (alt haps projected)
from ancestra.synteny import SyntenyContext, gene_positions

# (a) own adjacency (positions from data/genomes/<set>/*.parquet; shuffled control via shuffle_seed)
cc, oo, cov = gene_positions(gt.names, gt.species_of, gt.species, "data/genomes/orthobench")
ctx = SyntenyContext(cc, oo, family=fam, w=5, mode="multiplicative", lam=0.05,
                     anchor="amb", amb=0.9)            # shuffle_seed=11 -> negative control
res = run_merge(gt, tree, st, cfg, context=ctx)

# (b) or the core's adjacency (NodeContext.adjacency from run_merge(gene_order=...))
ctx = SyntenyContext(None, None, family=fam, mode="multiplicative", lam=0.05)
res = run_merge(gt, tree, st, cfg, context=ctx,
                gene_order=to_gene_order(gt, "data/genomes/orthobench"), adjacency_w=5)
ctx.stats   # per node: n_cand, n_anchors, n_adj_edges, n_supported
```

(a) and (b) give identical merges on the toy, which is a cross-check of the two adjacency builds.

**Modes** (the bonus is added to the cross-child candidate score before R1 to R3):
- `off`
- `tiebreak`: 1e-6 · support.
- `additive`: λ · min(s, cap)/cap.
- `multiplicative`: score · λ · min(s, cap)/cap.
- `override` (harmful on the toy, §5): a candidate gets the minimal bonus that makes it mutual-best only if, for one endpoint, its sequence score is ≥ `near` × that endpoint's best and its support exceeds the support of that best by ≥ `margin`. All other candidates get 0.

Lower-level functions: `context_support`, `select_anchors`, `AncestralAdjacency`, `pair_context_scores` and `rbh_anchors`.

**Costs.**
- Neighbour lists are O(N·w) once.
- Per node, unit adjacency is O(clade neighbour pairs), and support is O(E_cand · deg).
- On the toy, def32 (33k genes, 31 nodes) takes 8 to 10 s per full merge with context, against about 1 s without. Most of that is numba JIT and the per-node rebuild of unit adjacency. Incremental contraction of the adjacency along the merge map is the planned optimisation for QfO.

### 8.3 Findings
1. **The keep rule (DESIGN §7) passes.** Δ(real − shuffled) > 0 with CI > 0, and Δ(real − off) ≫ seed spread.
   - It holds for every multiplicative config at w ≤ 10, including the phase-1 default (w5, λ0.1): +0.007 [+0.001, +0.014].
   - The shuffled arms are null: pair F1 0.716 to 0.730, with Δ(shuffled − off) CIs that include 0.
   - The best arm, `real_w10_l0.5_coreanch`, gives **+0.046 [+0.019, +0.072]** (P +0.070, R +0.027).
   - It was selected among 34 dev configs, so expect shrinkage on test. A conservative choice is `real_w10_l0.3` (+0.032), which sits on the plateau.
2. **Teleost WGD pairs gain most:** vert–teleost +0.071 to +0.092 (CI > 0), teleost–teleost +0.044 to +0.058 (CI includes 0; only 113 ortholog pairs). Mammal–mammal gains +0.038, mostly precision (+0.056 P); its CI lower bound is about 0. Vert–invert does not change (+0.007, CI includes 0), as expected from the standalone benchmark.
3. **R1-only beats additive-everywhere.** `ctx_mode="all"` buys precision (+0.062 P) with no recall, and has lower F1 than R1-only at the same λ. Tiebreak (1e-6·support) never fires: there are no exact ties.
4. **Settings.**
   - w=10 is best in the merge; w=20 is +0.017 to +0.021 with a CI that touches 0.
   - Gains saturate at λ ≈ 0.5 (λ=1.0 is the same).
   - A ctx_window of 0.5, 0.75 or 0.9 hardly matters.
   - More anchors are better: core strict 1:1 (`anchor="core"`), or amb=0.95, beats the more conservative amb 0.9 and 0.8.
5. **OG-level F does not move with pair F1.** Strong arms show 66.4 to 66.7 against 68.1 off. But the *shuffled* seeds alone span 66.9 to 69.8 while their pair F1 is unchanged, so OG F on 34 dev RefOGs is dominated by a few root-level membership flips. It is unsuitable for S3/S5 decisions; use the pair metric.

### 8.4 Proposed merge-rule changes (for the core owner, via the PI)
1. **Default S5 config:** `ctx_mode="r1"`, `ctx_window=0.75`, `SyntenyContext(mode="multiplicative", lam=0.3–0.5, w=10, anchor="core")`. Lock it before any test peek, preferably λ=0.3 on the plateau.
2. **Use synteny in R3 fission-repair partner choice too.** It currently uses raw scores only. R3 attaches units to groups with no member of that child, which is the same partner-choice problem.
3. **Precision veto in R2/R3 instead of a bonus.** The ctx_all result (P +0.06) shows that context recognises hidden paralogy. Block an in-paralog absorption or a repair join when the joining unit has support 0 to the group's partner in the other child but ≥2 to a competing candidate. That captures the precision without the recall loss of inflating cross-child scores.
4. **Adopt the pair-level dev metric** (this script) alongside OG F for S3/S5 decisions. Δ(OG F) has a ±1.4-point null spread here.
5. Pass the adjacency `n_species` weights at deep nodes (later; deep nodes are where vert–invert fails, and synteny cannot help there).
